# Detective Story - Design Concept

## Design Philosophy

**You're Sherlock Holmes solving mysteries with Watson (AI partner).**

Clinical diagnosis = detective work. Mystery-solving framework removes test anxiety and makes curiosity the driver.

## Key Features

- **Case File Aesthetic**: Styled like detective's notebook with evidence logs
- **Watson Sidebar**: AI partner interjects with clues ("Holmes, notice the timeline here...")
- **Tools as Investigation Methods**: "Interview Patient", "Examine Evidence", "Request Forensics"
- **Clues Gathered**: Auto-populates with key findings as you investigate
- **Deduction Phase**: "Present your theory, Holmes" instead of "Submit diagnosis"
- **Solved/Unsolved Counter**: Cases solved vs mysteries remaining

## What Problem Does It Solve?

**Primary Issue**: Test anxiety and fear of being wrong paralyze users.

**Solution**: Reframes as mystery-solving with a partner. Mistakes aren't failures—they're part of the investigation. Watson makes you feel like you're collaborating, not performing.

## User Testing Questions

1. **Does curiosity replace anxiety?** Do users seem more exploratory?
2. **Do they engage with Watson?** Do they read his comments?
3. **Does the mystery framing change the tone?** Do they mention "solving" vs "diagnosing"?
4. **Is it playful without being silly?** Does it feel professional yet engaging?

## Design Details

- **Color Palette**: Moody mystery tones (#1a1614, #2d2620) with gold accents (#c9a962, #d4af37)
- **Typography**: Georgia serif for elegance, Courier for case files
- **Layout**: Case file on left, Watson on right, evidence log style
- **Tone**: Intriguing, collaborative, Sherlock BBC aesthetic

## Best For

Curious, story-driven learners who respond to narrative framing. Those who like puzzle games, mystery novels, and investigative shows.
